What runs without anyone watching

Which moments get a notification

Signing up is the loudest one. It isn't the only one — and not all of them deserve to wake up a channel.

whenSomeone signs up or starts a trial
Ping · hello · a call task. The one moment that gets all three — the chain above.
whenSomeone starts signing up and stops
Task in the morning, no 2am ping. A half-finished form is the person closest to buying, so somebody still follows it up.
whenSomeone asks for a human, or the bot loops
Instant ping, jumps the queue. Out of hours the widget says so honestly and offers the two things that work: book, or raise a ticket.
whenA call is booked — or nobody shows up
Task, reminder, task again. Today a booking is invisible to the panel, so a no-show costs us the customer and we never find out.
whenA call ends
Summary and follow-up drafted. The recap email that never went becomes a task instead of a good intention.
whenTrial day three · trial about to end
Task only, no ping. Logged in six times, never made an invoice — that's the call that decides whether they pay.
whenA ticket passes a working day
Nudge the owner, not the room. Broadcasting everything to everyone is how a channel turns into background noise.

Two rules that keep it safe

Transcripts never train the bot directly

Raw calls contain guesses, one customer's edge case, and their private data. Only approved cards become knowledge. That's about 5 cards a day, not 500.

Every card has an owner and a date

Ashish already flagged the real bug: when the screen changes, the doc doesn't, and the bot confidently teaches the old version. A card that can go stale quietly is the thing we're replacing.

Start with chat, not calls. Every chat ever had is already sitting in the support panel — no vendor, no consent forms, no calendars. We can mine cards from it this week. Calls join the loop once recording is sorted (next tab).

Three loops, not one

You asked whether the AI keeps handing us improvement opportunities — for the calls, and for our data. Yes. It's cleanest as three loops, because each one has a different owner and a different pace.

week 4

1 · The bot gets smarter

Reads chats, then transcripts. Writes a Q&A card with an article and a video. Juhi presses yes. That's the loop above.

needs transcripts

2 · The calls get better

Reads last week's calls. Writes one page: the objections that came up most, the answers that landed, what to say instead. The callers read it Monday morning.

needs the thread

3 · The product gets fixed

Reads every thread at once. Writes a shortlist: the screen generating the most tickets, the real reasons trials don't convert, the accounts that look like they're leaving. You decide what's worth building.

Every one of them is a suggestion on a page, once a week. Nothing in these three loops sends an email, changes an article or writes to a customer on its own. Same safety catch as the Approve button, applied to advice instead of answers.

Loop 3 is also the honest reason to build the thread first: an AI can only spot a pattern across customers if somebody wrote the history down.

What the AI is allowed to read

Worth being exact, because “our data, including the user data” can mean two very different things — and only one of them is ours to hand to a model.

✓ How people use Sole

Chats, call transcripts, tickets, what someone clicked, when they last logged in, where they got stuck, whether they converted. This is the material all three loops read.

✗ Our customers' books

Invoices, bank feeds, wages, tax. That's their money, not our training data, and it never enters an improvement loop. Being able to say that plainly is worth more to us than anything we'd learn from it.

Names and numbers get stripped at the approval step, so nothing customer-identifying survives into a published card.

And yes — it feeds the Command Centre

One direction only. Things get written to the thread; the Command Centre reads it. No second pipeline, and no two dashboards that disagree about the same week.

  • Time to first human contact — the promise, measured, not remembered.
  • Share of new signups spoken to before their first session ends.
  • Calls booked, made, missed — by caller, and by segment.
  • How much the bot handles alone, and the ten questions it still can't.
  • Cards approved, and how many people stopped asking after each one.
  • Tickets open, and anything older than a working day.
  • Trial → paid, split by whether a human ever spoke to them.
That last line is the one that pays for all of this. Nobody can produce that number today. Once we can, “should we call trial customers?” stops being an opinion.

Record the call. Don't build Zoom.

Jo wants every session recorded, transcribed and fed back to the AI. Agreed — and we can have it without writing a single line of video code.

Why not build our own video tool

Video is a commodity and a tar pit — media servers, echo cancellation, mobile, bad wifi. It would eat the whole runway on the one part that makes us zero difference. And customers won't install a new call app from their accounting software. They already accept Google Meet.
